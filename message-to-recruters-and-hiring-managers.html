A Message to Recruiters and Hiring Managers

Rate

$300 per hour. Prorated. Billed in 15-minute increments. Applies to every interview, screening call, and consultation.

This rate exists to signal one thing: only serious inquiries should reach out. Messaging recruiters and hiring teams takes time from my current technical work, my projects, and my self-study pursued on my own hours. That time has value. I am no longer giving it away.

Policy

I am setting my terms before anyone's time is wasted.

I expect compensation for all interview time. Screening calls included.

I will not participate in traditional coding interviews. LeetCode, HackerRank, data structures, algorithms — I received some training in this during my graduate degrees. However, I spent the majority of my time studying reinforcement learning, They do not measure reinforcement learning expertise, and asking my to do these assesments is asking a fish to climb a tree.

I will not complete take-home assignments or bounties. No unpaid trial periods. No "prove yourself first" work samples.

I will not accept entry-level positions. The rhetoric of "starting from the bottom" does not apply to me. My seven years of doctoral work is my starting from the bottom. PhD salaries are extremely low for the volume of work done. Grading and TA responsibilities drain energy that should go to research. Balancing all of it is exhausting.

Background

I spent seven years pursuing a PhD in robotics, specializing in reinforcement learning. I mastered out. I am a reinforcement learning researcher and engineer, not a general software engineer.

I am not someone who went into the tech industry after college. I spent seven years accepting opportunity cost while the majority of my peers reached senior levels in industry. Those years mattered. They taught me deep technical competence in a field where most competent practitioners need doctoral-level training.

On Snobbery and Rigor

I hear from many recruiters that there is a concern of lack of industry experience or work on industrial robots.

The techniques and engineering thinking still apply, despite the academic setting. I have experience in modeling of general phyical phenomena, including robot behavior on less-accurate hardawre. If I can accurately model and control a robot to balance in delicate states using lower-quality and budget-hardware, then I will definietly be able to so on more expensive hardware.

I could speak condescendingly about the lack of rigor and deep thinking in some industry settings. I could negate companies for not understanding the field. Though that is not respectful. Diversity of thought and background deserves valuation.

I am asking for the same consideration in return.

The institution of work should not undervalue this path. The snobbery needs to stop.
